EDTO in brief

EDTO (Extended Diversion Time Operations) is flight beyond 60 minutes from an adequate en-route alternate, inside the approved maximum diversion time. For the MAS B737-800 that is 120 minutes.

60 min
420 NM
120 min
820 NM
Basis
Still air, ISA, one engine out, M0.79 / 310 kt, 75,000 kg

A flight is non-EDTO when the whole route stays inside 60 minute circles round adequate airports. Where the route leaves those circles there is an EDTO segment, and EDTO rules apply to the flight.

Terms

Maximum diversion time
120, 180 or 207 min as approved. It sizes the area of operation only. It is not a time limit on an actual diversion.
Maximum diversion distance
Distance covered in that time, still air and ISA, at the one-engine-out speed schedule, including the descent to diversion altitude.
Area of operations
Circles of maximum diversion distance round the adequate airports. The route must stay inside them.
EEP · entry point
On the route, one hour (still air, ISA, one-engine-out speed) from the last adequate airport. Start of the EDTO segment.
EXP · exit point
Where the route comes back within one hour of an adequate airport. End of the EDTO segment.
EDP · equal distance point
On the route, the same distance from both en-route alternates.
ETP · equal time point
On the route, the same flying time to both en-route alternates. Same as the EDP in nil wind.

Diversion strategies

Standard
M0.79 / 280 kt, LRC ceiling. Use when fuel is critical.
Fixed speed
M0.79 / 310 kt. The speed the EDTO area and the critical fuel chart are based on.
Obstacle
Max continuous thrust at minimum manoeuvring speed (driftdown) until the obstacle is cleared, then standard or fixed speed.

Alternate renomination

Needed when an en-route alternate becomes unsuitable after departure (weather, NOTAM, closure). Pick the new airport from the EDTO area of operations chart, then use the FMC. Never press EXEC during this work.

  1. Suitability. Check latest weather and NOTAMs for the new alternates (ACARS if available).
  2. EDP. Find the point equal in distance to both alternates.
    • Enter EA1, then EA2, on the last LEGS page. Do not execute. Note the track and distance.
    • Type EA2/-half distance between them to make the halfway fix.
    • Move the halfway fix to the FIX page, then erase EA1 and EA2 from LEGS.
    • Enter the track +90° if the route is to the right, −90° if to the left.
    • Line-select that radial and put it in LEGS on the leg it crosses. That waypoint is the EDP.
  3. ETP. Find the point equal in time to both alternates.
    • Check the winds are in RTE DATA.
    • Put EA1 under the EDP. Do not execute.
    • Read its ETA on RTE DATA, then ERASE.
    • Repeat for EA2.
    • Calculate the shift: ETA difference ÷ 2 × NM per min (8 NM/min at 480 kt TAS, so difference × 4).
    • Move the EDP into the wind: EDP/±NM (tailwind −, headwind +).
    • Recheck both ETAs. Adjust 1 or 2 NM until they match.
  4. Critical fuel by FMC. See below.
  5. Critical fuel by chart. See below.
For datalink or CPDLC, move the ETP to the FIX page: only published waypoints should stay in LEGS.

Critical fuel check · FMC

A quick cross-check of fuel on arrival at the alternate from the ETP, depressurised. PROGRESS is next to EXEC: go slowly.

  1. RTE page 1: enter the alternate as DEST. Do not execute.
  2. CRZ page: set the strategy speed. Fuel critical = standard strategy, 280 kt.
  3. DES page: speed 280, delete the 10,000 ft speed restriction.
  4. LEGS: at the ETP enter 280/10000.
  5. Put the alternate right under the ETP as the last waypoint, with /1500.
  6. Optional, for a better descent path: ETP/50 placed after the ETP.
  7. PROGRESS: fuel at the alternate must be above 2,400 kg.

Critical fuel check · chart

  1. QRH Ops Info: engine inoperative 310 KIAS critical fuel reserves.
  2. Weight at the new ETP = ZFW + (fuel on board − fuel to burn to the ETP), or from the OFP.
  3. Enter with the distance from ETP to the alternate and the wind. Apply the ISA and icing corrections.
  4. Compare fuel on board at the ETP with the critical fuel. The handout asks for equal or more.
  5. If it is less, make sure arrival fuel is above minimum diversion fuel (MDF).
OM-A 8.5.9.8: there is no EDTO rule that you must reach the critical point with fuel at or above the critical fuel, and no diversion is required for that alone. Normal fuel policy applies. See the OMA tab.

1 Planning · the airports

Adequate 8.5.6.2
Landing performance is met at the expected weight, and at the time of use the airport is open with ATC or CTAF, navaids, weather reporting, lighting, emergency services, at least one let-down aid (ILS, VOR/DME, VOR or NDB), and fire cover equal to RFFS category 4 (30 minutes notice is acceptable for an EDTO alternate).

EDTO weather planning minima. 8.5.8.2
  • B737 cannot use CAT II / III alternate minima: no autoland with one engine out.
  • Wind plus gust must be within limits, including the crosswind limit, and the runway condition forecast counts.
  • A PROB40 or TEMPO below minima must be taken into account. Probabilities below 30% are ignored. 8.5.8.7
  • MEL items that raise approach minima also raise the EDTO alternate minima.
  • Departure and destination need not meet EDTO alternate minima unless they are also used as EDTO alternates.

2 Planning · period of suitability

The forecast must stay at or above EDTO minima from 1 hour before the earliest ETA to 1 hour after the latest ETA at each EDTO alternate. The OFP prints the period (“ENRTE ALTNS, weather suitability period”). 8.5.8.3

Earliest ETA
Two-engine diversion from the first ETP for that airport, at normal cruise level and speed.
Latest ETA
Two-engine diversion from the second ETP for that airport, at FL100 or MORA, at LRC (depressurised only).
Delay over 1 hour: the period must be worked out again and checked against the forecast.

Hybrid EDTO: if only one alternate of the pair is suitable (the other adequate but below minima), the segment may be planned on that one airport, provided its maximum diversion distance covers the whole EDTO segment. 8.5.8.3

3 Planning · fuel

The area of operation is still air and ISA. Fuel is not: it uses the forecast wind and temperature, for a failure at the critical point (CP). On the OFP the CP may appear as the ETP between two EDTO alternates. 8.5.8.5

Scenario at the CPProfile
Engine failure onlyNever fuel critical (higher levels). Not normally computed.
Depressurisation,
two engines		Emergency descent to FL100 or MORA, cruise at LRC.
Engine failure +
depressurisation		Emergency descent to FL100 or MORA, cruise at the EDTO speed (310 kt).

The critical fuel is the higher of the last two. It includes: 8.1.7.7

  • Burn from the CP to the alternate in the worst case
  • 5% of that burn as contingency
  • 5% fuel mileage penalty, or the demonstrated performance factor
  • 15 minutes holding at 1,500 ft over the alternate
  • An approach, a go-around and a second approach
  • Any MEL / CDL effect, and APU burn if it is needed as a power source
  • If icing is forecast (OAT below −9 °C at the CP or the alternate): engine and wing anti-ice, and ice on unheated surfaces 8.5.8.8
If the EDTO plan needs more than the standard plan, the extra shows as EDTO / ADDNL in the OFP fuel summary.

4 Crew preparation

  1. Check the required EDTO alternates. Confirm each is adequate from NOTAMs and SNOWTAMs.
  2. Check each alternate’s period of validity on the OFP, and that the forecast ceiling and visibility are above EDTO minima for that period.
  3. Check the OFP critical fuel. Minimum sector fuel is the higher of the EDTO fuel plan and the standard fuel plan.
  4. If any of these is not met: a new OFP, new EDTO alternates and new calculations are required.
  5. Technical log: no open defect affecting EDTO status. Check the MEL for EDTO limits (“for ER operations” / “except for ER operations”). An aircraft that cannot comply is placarded NON-EDTO. 8.5.7
  6. Cockpit preparation and EDTO system checks per OM-B. After start, cross-check fuel on board before take-off.
ATC clearances. The PIC must not accept a clearance that takes the aircraft outside the approved EDTO envelope, in distance or flight level. 8.5.9.1
Briefing folder for an EDTO flight: OFP with EEP / ETP / EXP and fuel plans, weather for the route and all EDTO alternates, NOTAMs for all en-route alternates. 8.5.8.12

5 Before the entry point (EEP)

  1. Get a weather update for the declared EDTO alternates (METAR, TAF, SIGMET, SPECI), with dispatch or by your own means, plus any new NOTAM or SNOWTAM.
  2. Weather at the ETA at each alternate must be above company or crew minima.
  3. If an alternate is below those minima or not available: reassess the area of operation on the remaining suitable airports, and consider a re-route or turn-back.
  4. The PIC confirms the decision: continue, re-route or turn back.
Airborne, the EDTO dispatch minima no longer apply. The test before the EEP is normal company or crew landing minima, not the +200 ft / +400 ft planning minima. 8.5.9.6

6 In the EDTO segment

Weather 8.5.9.6
Keep updating the en-route alternates’ weather. After the EEP there is no requirement to change the flight if the weather drops below minima.
Fuel 8.5.9.8
Normal company fuel monitoring applies. There is no EDTO rule that you must reach the CP with fuel on board at or above the OFP critical fuel. The CP is not a reclearance point. If the estimate at the CP is lower, no diversion is required as long as estimated fuel at destination covers the diversion to the destination alternate. Minimise consumption.
Critical fuel in flight
Base it on actual conditions. The OFP figure may include an icing reserve that is not needed if no icing is forecast.
ETP on the OFP 8.5.8.4
It may not show equal times to the two airports, because the OFP’s critical scenario is usually depressurisation, not engine failure alone.

When to consider a re-route or diversion 8.5.10.1

  • An alternate falls below company or crew minima, or becomes unsuitable, before the EEP
  • Loss of the required RNP capability before entering the area
  • A failure that requires landing at the nearest en-route alternate
  • A failure that increases fuel burn beyond the reserves available

Diversion strategy 8.5.10.3

Fixed speed
The speed the area of operation is built on. Expected after an engine failure, in-flight fire or other warning, when fuel is not critical.
Standard (LRC)
Near minimum fuel burn. Use when fuel is critical.
Obstacle
Best height keeping at very low speed, where terrain or a height restriction exists.
PIC authority. The PIC may choose or deviate from any strategy and speed after assessing the situation, even if the diversion then takes longer than the approved diversion time. Watch the fuel when deviating from the planned speed. 8.5.10.4

1 Before departure and after airborne

  • Test the crossfeed valve. Selector OPEN: the VALVE OPEN light goes bright, then dim. Selector CLOSE: bright, then out. Enter any failure in the Technical Log. OI.4.1
  • Insert the EDTO alternates into FMC FIX INFO, then verify them. OI.4.1
  • Send the EDTO message once airborne, to FDC by ACARS or SATCOM (or the EDTO prompt if fitted). It carries: ATD · TOW · TOF · EEP · ETP · EXP · ETA OI.4.1

2 Before the EEP

  • Review aircraft position. ANP must be better than RNP. OI.4.1
  • Check aircraft status: master caution recall, and review the EDTO restrictions. EDTO critical equipment is listed in the SOM. OI.4.1
  • APU (choose one). OI.4.2
    • APU on demand: keep the APU OFF. B737-800 9M-MXA to 9M-MXY, and all B737-8.
    • Non-APU on demand: START the APU and verify the APU GEN OFF bus light is on. B737-800 9M-FFF and 9M-MLK to 9M-MLV.
  • Check the fuel situation. Forecast fuel over the ETP should be above the critical fuel required. If it is below, compare arrival fuel with MDF and make sure it is above MDF to continue into EDTO. OI.4.2
  • Check the EDTO fuel alternate with the flow chart below. OI.4.2
Compare with OM-A 8.5.9.8. OM-A says there is no EDTO rule to reach the critical point with fuel at or above the critical fuel, and that no diversion is required for that alone. The QRH asks you to check arrival fuel against MDF when it is below. Both are on the OMA tab.

3 Alternate check (flow chart)

  1. Check updated weather and NOTAMs for the nominated EDTO alternates, and that the airport charts are accessible. OI.4.3
  2. If the alternates satisfy the criteria: brief, and continue into EDTO.
  3. If not: request a new alternate from FDC, or re-nominate manually, and check fuel.
  4. If the new alternates satisfy the criteria: brief, and continue into EDTO.
  5. If not: reroute to non-EDTO or divert, and advise FDC.

4 Re-nominating an alternate

When a re-nomination is needed, send a downlink to FDC by ACARS, or call FDC by SATCOM. Ask for: OI.4.4

  • The new EA
  • The EEP, ETP and EXP coordinates and the EDTO critical fuel
  • A weather and NOTAM update

The new waypoints FDC uplinks may be inserted in the FMC. Brief the changes.

5 EDTO briefing

  1. Decide the diversion airport. Enter the alternates in FMC FIX INFO and/or the ALTN page. FMC route 2 is recommended. OI.4.4
  2. Decide the diversion routing. Look at both the direct track (ALTN page) and routes via airways. Route 2 is recommended.
  3. Check MORA / MEA on the chosen routing.
  4. FMC EO CRZ page: check the maximum altitude for obstacle (driftdown ceiling).
  5. QRH Ops Info: check the maximum altitude for standard (LRC ceiling, 280 kt / M.79). PI section: check the maximum altitude and speed for standard (LRC ceiling).
  6. QRH Ops Info: check the maximum altitude for fixed speed (310 kt / M.79 ceiling).
  7. Decide the strategy: obstacle, standard or fixed speed. Fuel critical: standard. Time critical: fixed speed.
  8. Brief the scenarios that need a diversion to the EDTO alternate, for example engine failure or fire, rapid decompression, smoke or fumes or fire without thrust loss. OI.4.5

The QRH separates two cases: OI.4.5

Descent required, or unable to maintain altitude
For example thrust loss or engine failure, rapid depressurisation.
Descent not required, or able to maintain altitude
For example no thrust loss, medical, cargo fire.

6 In the EDTO segment and after the EXP

  • Monitor the status of the en-route diversion airports. OI.4.5
  • Approaching each ETP, review the EDTO briefing again for the next diversion airport.
  • After the EXP: if the APU is on, switch it off for the last hour of cruise.
  • After the EXP: test the crossfeed valve again (OPEN: light bright then dim; CLOSE: bright then out) and report any failure in the Technical Log.

Critical fuel reserves · OI.4.7

310 KIAS cruise critical fuel reserves, engine inoperative, B737-800. OI.4.7

Swipe sideways for the weight columns.
Weights are the weight at the critical point. Fuel in 1,000 kg. Add the wind correction to the nil-wind fuel (tailwind −, headwind +). A dash means the chart gives no value.

Based on

  • Emergency descent to 10,000 ft, level cruise at 10,000 ft
  • 250 KIAS descent to 1,500 ft, 15 minute hold at 1,500 ft
  • One missed approach, then approach and land
  • 5% allowance for wind errors. Includes APU fuel burn.

Corrections

  • Increase fuel required by 0.5% for each 10 °C above ISA.
  • If icing is expected, increase fuel required by 17%: engine and wing anti-ice (5%) and ice on unheated surfaces (12%).
  • Allowance for performance deterioration is not included.
Compare, then use the higher. Compare this chart with the critical fuel reserves for all engines operative and use the higher of the two. The all-engines chart is not in the app.
