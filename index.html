EDTO in brief

EDTO (Extended Diversion Time Operations) is flight beyond 60 minutes from an adequate en-route alternate, inside the approved maximum diversion time. For the MAS B737-800 that is 120 minutes.

60 min
420 NM
120 min
820 NM
Basis
Still air, ISA, one engine out, M0.79 / 310 kt, 75,000 kg

A flight is non-EDTO when the whole route stays inside 60 minute circles round adequate airports. Where the route leaves those circles there is an EDTO segment, and EDTO rules apply to the flight.

Terms

Maximum diversion time
120, 180 or 207 min as approved. It sizes the area of operation only. It is not a time limit on an actual diversion.
Maximum diversion distance
Distance covered in that time, still air and ISA, at the one-engine-out speed schedule, including the descent to diversion altitude.
Area of operations
Circles of maximum diversion distance round the adequate airports. The route must stay inside them.
EEP · entry point
On the route, one hour (still air, ISA, one-engine-out speed) from the last adequate airport. Start of the EDTO segment.
EXP · exit point
Where the route comes back within one hour of an adequate airport. End of the EDTO segment.
EDP · equal distance point
On the route, the same distance from both en-route alternates.
ETP · equal time point
On the route, the same flying time to both en-route alternates. Same as the EDP in nil wind.

Diversion strategies

Standard
M0.79 / 280 kt, LRC ceiling. Use when fuel is critical.
Fixed speed
M0.79 / 310 kt. The speed the EDTO area and the critical fuel chart are based on.
Obstacle
Max continuous thrust at minimum manoeuvring speed (driftdown) until the obstacle is cleared, then standard or fixed speed.

Alternate renomination

Needed when an en-route alternate becomes unsuitable after departure (weather, NOTAM, closure). Pick the new airport from the EDTO area of operations chart, then use the FMC. Never press EXEC during this work.

  1. Suitability. Check latest weather and NOTAMs for the new alternates (ACARS if available).
  2. EDP. LEGS last page: enter EA1 then EA2, note track and distance. Type EA2/-half distance between them to make the halfway fix. Put it on the FIX page and erase EA1 and EA2 from LEGS. Enter the track +90° if the route is to the right, −90° if to the left. Line-select that radial and put it in LEGS on the leg it crosses. That waypoint is the EDP.
  3. ETP. Check winds are in RTE DATA. Put EA1 under the EDP, read its ETA on RTE DATA, ERASE. Same for EA2. Shift = ETA difference ÷ 2 × NM per min (8 NM/min at 480 kt TAS, so difference × 4). Move the EDP into the wind with EDP/±NM. Recheck; adjust 1 or 2 NM until the ETAs match.
  4. Critical fuel by FMC. See below.
  5. Critical fuel by chart. See below.
For datalink or CPDLC, move the ETP to the FIX page: only published waypoints should stay in LEGS.

Critical fuel check · FMC

A quick cross-check of fuel on arrival at the alternate from the ETP, depressurised. PROGRESS is next to EXEC: go slowly.

  1. RTE page 1: enter the alternate as DEST. Do not execute.
  2. CRZ page: set the strategy speed. Fuel critical = standard strategy, 280 kt.
  3. DES page: speed 280, delete the 10,000 ft speed restriction.
  4. LEGS: at the ETP enter 280/10000.
  5. Put the alternate right under the ETP as the last waypoint, with /1500.
  6. Optional, for a better descent path: ETP/50 placed after the ETP.
  7. PROGRESS: fuel at the alternate must be above 2,400 kg.

Critical fuel check · chart

  1. QRH Ops Info: engine inoperative 310 KIAS critical fuel reserves.
  2. Weight at the new ETP = ZFW + (fuel on board − fuel to burn to the ETP), or from the OFP.
  3. Enter with the distance from ETP to the alternate and the wind. Apply the ISA and icing corrections.
  4. Fuel on board at the ETP must be equal to or more than the critical fuel.
  5. If it is less, make sure arrival fuel is above minimum diversion fuel (MDF).
